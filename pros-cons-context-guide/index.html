Pros & Cons Context Guide

Why pros and cons must map to a clear scope, time, usage situation, and evidence.

A list is not a conclusion

Pros and cons are only meaningful inside a scope. Without one, they are preferences dressed as findings.

Scope

Which product, version, and situation does the list cover? A con for one situation is not a con for another.

Time

When was the list written? A product that changed may have changed its pros and cons too.

Usage situation

Under what conditions was each point observed? A “con” only holds in the situation it came from.

Evidence

What supports each point? A point without evidence is an assertion, not a finding.

?Open item: is any pro or con being read without its scope, time, situation, or evidence?

A worked read

  1. List: a page lists pros and cons.
  2. Scope: no product version or situation is stated.
  3. Result: the list stays an opinion, not a conclusion.
